I started creating content in February 2026. I had been thinking about it since November 2025, and I had already been writing a lot on LinkedIn since 2024. To me, the promise has been kept: I receive engagement proposals that do not come from intermediaries, but from the teams themselves.

These are people who have not written down their need yet. I can talk to them directly, build the engagement end to end and define its objectives. That is a luxury not every freelancer gets.

## What content brought

I stayed on short content. By mid-July, the counter showed more than 400 posts, 527,000 views and impressions and 22,000 interactions, across all platforms.

I tried long form with a podcast test. It was not conclusive for me: too much preparation. I need something more interactive, something I can do with a clear head. That is my perfectionism again, which I am trying to reduce. I learned to ship a piece of content as fast as possible, without stopping at the visual quality or the cleanliness of the cuts.

Revenue is positive, thankfully.

## Content is not enough

Publishing is not enough to be well positioned. There is a network effect. You have to be passionate, keep up with the field, know how to put the people you meet at ease, talk about their problems, stay curious instead of thinking only about your own needs.

I have enormous respect for the work of salespeople in consulting firms. They have skills a freelancer should learn from. We should train, learn to make cold calls, maintain the relationship with a client even when we are not on an engagement with them, check in. With AI, even just scheduling the reminders to do it has become easy.

## Six months with agents

When I was at Enedis and talked about MCP and agentic systems, people looked at me wide-eyed. I picked the subject back up for good in May, on an agentic SRE project at a large account. I took the harness apart, and I use it every day.

Since June, that makes 3,836 sessions with a coding agent, over 166 distinct days. Added up, around 2,250 hours of agent activity, with agents often running several at a time in parallel. On GitHub, 1,400 contributions in 2026, almost all since June. That is how I wrote [an implementation of OpAMP in Rust](/en/blog/opamp-rust-protocole-public/), rebuilt my websites and put service offerings online with appointment booking.

I also discovered what context-switching fatigue is. That is what made me understand the value of permanent agents and of the software factory: to stop being in the loop all the time, and stay close instead. And the models have improved impressively.

All of this made me mature on communication, on how I see my profession and on how to sell it.

## What did not work

Fixed-price work. I wanted to get out of the hell of selling time and sell results instead. Every fixed-price opportunity I had stopped at planning or at the quote. Like most people, I still depend on selling my time.

And there was an unpaid invoice. On an engagement that went through an intermediary, I had to go to court to obtain a document forcing the consulting firm to pay me for the days I had worked. I do not want to step into a conflict between a very large company and a consulting firm, that is not my problem. I did my days, I need to be paid. I mention it because any freelancer can face it, and it weighs on cash flow.

## My two bets for 2027

The first is the AIOps turn. I stay positioned on fleet management, and I keep a close watch on agentic systems and observability, so I can deploy autonomous agents at clients that are able to resolve incidents, or to make resolving them easier.

The second is solo dev. Ship as many projects as possible and test them, without waiting for them to be perfect. And above all work on what I master least: marketing, go-to-market, product-market fit, MRR and the whole vocabulary of customer acquisition.

## My conviction

Nothing is certain. Everything can change from one moment to the next. The ones who will get something good out of it are the minds that are not rigid, that have elasticity and take risks, while staying consistent with their values.

What matters most to me, as to everyone, is spending as much time as possible with the people close to me. That is what AI is for in my life: freeing up time, removing friction and automating as many administrative tasks as possible.
