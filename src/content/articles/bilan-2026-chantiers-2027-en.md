I spent part of 2026 working on fleet management, a Rust implementation of OpAMP and my development workflow with agents. Those projects keep bringing me back to practical questions: which state is known, which evidence is missing, and who needs to take over?

This review is being prepared before the end of the year. It does not claim to describe December in advance. Final results and 2027 priorities still need to be grounded in what was actually delivered and observed.

## A protocol to share, a product to keep private

For OpAMP, I intend to publish the Rust protocol implementation. The product and its frontend remain private. That boundary helps define the contribution I want others to use: behavior they can read and test, with identifiable limitations.

At publication time, the review must reflect what has actually become public. An intention to release code does not become a release announcement simply because an article has a date in the calendar.

## Recover time for the need itself

With development agents, I want to spend more time on requirements, business questions and architecture, and less on repetitive work. A completed execution does not measure that progress on its own. Restarts, handoffs and time spent discovering why a task stopped also count.

I would assess concrete situations: was a task waiting for a decision, technical evidence or a handoff that did not exist? That distinction helps avoid assuming that every obstacle calls for either more autonomy or more human approval.

## Directions to clarify for 2027

Observability remains my professional starting point. Tools change, but I still need to understand what a team can explain with its data and what it must reconstruct during an incident.

The directions already under consideration include fleet management around OpAMP, evaluating observability solutions and improving continuity when working with agents. These are areas to explore, rather than a dated roadmap or delivery promise.

Before setting priorities, I want to distinguish what was used, what was tested and what stayed in a plan. Decisions for 2027 should point to an actual constraint or user feedback. A useful review should recover the reasons to continue a project and acknowledge the reasons to reduce its scope.
